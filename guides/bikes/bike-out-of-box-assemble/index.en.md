This guide follows the quick assembly guide that Trek ships with its city bikes. Other brands and models go together in a similar order, but the details differ, so keep the instructions for your own bike at hand.

You will need tools: hex keys or a torque wrench for the handlebar and saddle bolts, and a 15 mm pedal wrench for the pedals. A torque wrench makes sure the bolts are tight enough.

> [!WARNING]
> Wrong assembly can lead to an accident. Use only the hardware supplied with the bike and, if you doubt anything, have the finished bike checked in a shop before you ride.

## Take the bike out and inspect it

![A bike in its cardboard box with a magnifier over the front fork](step-1.svg)

Take the bike out of the box and remove the cardboard around the fork. Check for shipping damage and that all parts are there. Pay special attention to the fork legs and the seat stays, inside and outside. If anything is damaged, contact the manufacturer or the shop. On an e-bike, switch the electric system off before assembly.

## Attach the handlebar to the stem

If the handlebar is not on yet, put it on the stem and align it. Do not tighten the bolts fully yet, you will adjust it and tighten later. Make sure the cables are not pinched, not routed over the stem and not between the faceplate and the stem. The handlebar may hang on the cables for a short while.

## Fit the front wheel

![The front wheel goes into the fork with the brake rotor centred and the axle lever highlighted](step-3.svg)

Remove the spacer between the brake pads and **do not squeeze the brake** afterwards: the pads would close and be hard to open again. Lift the front end, put the wheel into the fork and install the axle. Tighten it until the tool clicks or the lever leaves a noticeable impression in your palm. Centre the brake rotor. On rim brakes, squeeze the calipers with your hands and connect the front brake cable.

## Grease the thread and start the pedals by hand

![The pedal thread is greased and the pedal is started by hand, an arrow points forward](step-4.svg)

Grease the pedal thread and clean the thread in the crank arm: there may be glue left from the packing strap. Thread the pedal in **by hand**, turning it by the nut, not by the pedal body. Both pedals tighten toward the front of the bike, so the left pedal has a left-hand thread. Pedals are usually marked L and R: do not mix up the sides.

## Tighten the pedals with the wrench

![A pedal wrench on the pedal axle, tightening in the forward direction](step-5.svg)

Tighten each pedal with the pedal wrench until the tool leaves a noticeable impression in your palm. The chainring has sharp points: work carefully so you do not cut yourself.

## Inflate the tyres

![Two valve types, Schrader and Presta, with a pressure gauge next to them](step-6.svg)

Inflate both tyres to the pressure range printed on the tyre. Open the valve before inflating and close it again when you have finished.

## Insert the seatpost and adjust the saddle

![The seatpost sits in the frame past the minimum insertion mark, the saddle can be raised or lowered](step-7.svg)

Insert the seatpost into the frame so that the minimum insertion line disappears into the frame, and set the saddle height to suit you: at the bottom of the pedal stroke your knee should be slightly bent. Align the saddle with the top tube and tighten the clamp bolt or the quick release lever.

## Align the handlebar

Now that the front wheel is on, align the handlebar: loosen the faceplate bolts if needed, centre the bar and rotate it so the brake levers sit at about 45 degrees to the ground.

## Tighten the faceplate bolts in a cross pattern

![The four stem faceplate bolts are tightened in an X pattern, half a turn each](step-9.svg)

Tighten the faceplate bolts half a turn at a time in an X pattern until the torque wrench clicks. The gap on the top and bottom of the faceplate should be equal: if it is not, loosen the bolts and tighten them again in the same order.

## Run through the final checklist

Before and after the first ride go through Trek's checklist: is the front wheel installed correctly, are the pedals tight, are the tyres inflated, is the saddle at the right height, is the handlebar straight and are the faceplate bolts tight. Read the owner's manual from the box, register the bike and do a pre-ride check before every ride.
