A clean chain transfers your pedalling better and a drivetrain lasts longer. Shimano points out that regular chain care is not a time-consuming or laborious job. The order below is simple: degrease, rinse, dry and lube.

> [!WARNING]
> Keep degreaser and lube away from brake discs and brake pads.

## Set up the bike and the workspace

Put the bike in a stand or lean it against something stable so that you can turn the pedals backward. Lay down cardboard and put on gloves. If you have a chain wear indicator, check the chain now: a worn chain is better replaced than cleaned.

## Shift the chain onto the big front chainring

![Drivetrain from the side: the chain sits on the largest front chainring](step-2.svg)

Shimano suggests shifting onto the big chainring before cleaning: the chain is under more tension and is easier to work on with a brush.

## Spray degreaser on the chain and cassette

![Degreaser spray can aimed at the chain, droplets landing on the links](step-3.svg)

Apply a generous amount of spray degreaser or solvent to the chain and the cassette. Give it a few seconds to soak into the grime.

## Pedal backward and hold the brush against the chain

![Stiff brush held against the chain while the cranks turn backward](step-4.svg)

Hold the brush against the chain and cassette with one hand and turn the pedal backward with the other. The grime comes off every link and you do not have to move the brush.

## Brush all sides of the chain and the sprockets

Brush the chain on the front, back, top and bottom, then clean the teeth of the chainrings and the cassette where the chain touches them. The dirt should come off completely.

## Rinse with clean water

![A stream of clean water running over the chain and the cassette](step-6.svg)

Rinse the chain and the sprockets with clean water to wash away the degreaser and the loosened dirt.

## Wipe the chain and let it dry

Run the chain through a clean dry rag. Then let it dry completely before you lube it: lube does not go onto a wet chain well.

## Choose a lube for your weather

Dry lube (wax or Teflon) is cleaner and suits a dry climate, but you reapply it more often. Wet (oil based) lube stays on in rain and mud, but it collects more dirt. Choose by the conditions you ride in.

## Put a drop of lube on every roller

![A single drop of lube falling onto one roller of the chain](step-9.svg)

Put a small drop of lube on every roller while you turn the pedal backward slowly. Do not pour it on: the excess attracts dirt.

## Wipe off the excess lube

![A clean rag wrapped around the chain wiping off excess lube](step-10.svg)

Hold the chain in a clean dry rag and turn the pedals backward until the outside of the chain feels dry. The lube should stay inside the links.
