V-brakes are rim brakes: two arms with pads squeeze the wheel rim. Over time the pads wear and the cable stretches, and the brake starts to lag or rub. The adjustment has two parts: first the pads and arms, then the cable tension.

This guide is based on material from the bike maker Reid Cycles. Tightening torques and bolt sizes depend on your brake, so go by the bolt heads and the manual of your brake.

> [!WARNING]
> Before you ride, make sure the cable pinch bolt is tight. The brake depends on it.

## Check the wheel

Make sure the wheel is clamped properly and the quick release or the axle nuts are tight. If the wheel sits crooked, the brake will rub anyway.

## Inspect the cable guide

Check the metal cable guide on the brake arm. It can bend if the handlebar has spun around. If it is damaged, replace it and do not ride with a damaged part.

## Centre the pads on the rim

![The brake pads lie flat on the rim braking surface below the tyre](step-3.svg)

Set each pad so that it meets the rim squarely, both sides look the same, and the pad does not touch the tyre or hang below the rim. To do this loosen the pad bolt, move the pad and tighten it again.

## Even out the arms with the spring screw

![The small spring tension screw on each brake arm](step-4.svg)

If the arms return unevenly and one pad rubs on the rim, adjust the small spring tension screw on one or both arms. Keep the brake centred as you do it.

## Wind the barrel adjuster all the way in

![The barrel adjuster on the brake lever is wound fully in and highlighted](step-5.svg)

On the brake lever, turn the barrel adjuster all the way in. You will use it later for fine adjustment.

## Loosen the pinch bolt and take up the cable

![The cable pinch bolt on the brake arm and an arrow showing the cable being pulled](step-6.svg)

Loosen the cable pinch bolt on the brake and pull the cable to the tension you want. Leave a little room between the pads and the rim. Then tighten the pinch bolt.

## Test the brake

![The wheel spins freely, the lever is squeezed, a check mark beside it](step-7.svg)

Squeeze the lever and spin the wheel: it should turn freely without rubbing. If the wheel rubs or the lever brakes weakly, repeat the previous step and take up the cable again until the brake works as it should.

## Fine-tune with the barrel adjuster

For small changes use the barrel adjuster on the lever. Make sure the cable pinch bolt is tight. If the wheel rubs only at one spot, the rim is bent: take the bike to a shop.
