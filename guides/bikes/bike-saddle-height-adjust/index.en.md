You set the saddle height by your leg, then make sure the post is inserted deep enough and clamped firmly. It takes a few minutes with a hex key or a quick release lever.

## Check the current height with your leg

![Rider on the saddle with the heel on the lower pedal and the leg slightly bent](step-1.svg)

Sit on the saddle and put your heel on the lower pedal. According to Trek, the leg should be only slightly bent. If the knee is bent more than that, raise the saddle. If you cannot reach the pedal, lower it.

## Find the minimum insertion line on the post

The seatpost has a minimum insertion line marked on it. Do not raise the saddle so that this line shows above the frame: Trek says it can damage the post or the frame. If you cannot get the height you need that way, see a bike shop.

## Open the seatpost clamp

![Seat collar with a quick release lever being opened while the other hand holds the seatpost](step-3.svg)

With a quick release, open the lever with one hand and hold the post with the other, then wiggle it - it should move freely. With a bolt clamp, hold the saddle and turn the bolt counterclockwise with a hex key (usually 4, 5 or 6 mm, or T25).

## Set the height

![Seatpost raised in the seat tube so that its minimum insertion line stays hidden inside](step-4.svg)

Raise or lower the post to the height you want. The minimum insertion line must not be visible above the seat tube.

## Line the saddle up with the frame

![View from above: saddle nose lined up with the top tube along the centre line of the bike](step-5.svg)

Look along the bike and point the nose of the saddle along the top tube. The saddle should sit on the centre line of the bike.

## Close the clamp

![Seat collar bolt being tightened with a hex key](step-6.svg)

Close the quick release lever firmly, or turn the bolt clockwise to the torque marked on the clamp. If there is no marking, GT gives 7 Nm for seatpost bolts. Use a torque wrench if you can.

## Check that the saddle does not move

Take the nose and the back of the saddle and push and pull it up, down and sideways. It should not move or twist. If it does, realign it and tighten again.

## Test the height on a short ride

Ride a short loop. If your leg stretches for the pedal or the knee is too bent, adjust again. Check the clamp and the saddle bolts again after a few days: bolts loosen over time.
